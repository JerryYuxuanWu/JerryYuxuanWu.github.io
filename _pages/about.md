I am a second-year Ph.D. student in Statistics at the University of Toronto. I am graciously supervised by Dr. [Meredith Franklin](https://meredithfranklin.github.io/). I received my B.S. in Statistics and Economics also from the University of Toronto, an academic foundation I leverage to ground my statistical research in solving real-world problems.

My work combines statistical modelling, machine learning, and spatial-temporal data analysis to understand complex datasets and communicate actionable results. 

My current research projects surround unconventional oil and gas development in the Permian Basin, hydrogen sulfide exposure near industrial areas, and statistical methods for spatial and environmental data. Specifically, I work with Non-Negative Matrix Factorizations (NMF) and Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) to perform source apportionment and model environmental variables. 

My goal is to apply and extend state-of-the-art statistical methods to environmental data to help us uncover the truth about our home. Currently, I am researching the stability and identifiability of NMF, quantifying NMF factor importance, and assessing the impact of NMF model selection.

Outside of school, I have a pretty wide range of hobbies, from cooking to gaming to fishing to music to much more. I've found that exploring different interests really helps me think outside the box when it comes to learning. In fact, anytime I pick up a new statistical model, I almost always find myself trying to map it onto one of my hobbies just to see how it fits.
